Stakes by Kaleva

The scoreboard tells you the score; Stakes tells you what it was worth. After every goal it re-simulates the rest of the Liiga regular season, shows how every team's playoff chance moved, and an AI newsroom turns those numbers into short fan stories. Code checks every number before anything is published. Built in 24 hours for Kaleva Media's sports content challenge.

Problem

Sports sites report results but rarely explain consequences.

A goal in a mid-table game can swing a rival's playoff chance by ten points, and nobody tells that rival's fans. Kaleva Media's challenge asked for sports content that goes beyond the scoreboard.

AI could write that content cheaply, but language models invent numbers. A newspaper cannot publish a story with a made-up statistic, so any AI output had to be verifiable.

Solution

A probability engine plus a verified AI newsroom, delivered across the moments a fan cares about.

  • Before the gameWhat tonight is worth: each team's playoff chance after a win and after a loss.
  • During the gameAlerts such as "That goal cost Kärpät 10 points", even when your team isn't playing.
  • After the gameAn article widget: the playoff line jumps at every goal and the table re-sorts.
  • Between gamesA scenario explorer: pick upcoming results and see the odds change.
  • In the newsroomAn editor queue showing what the checker held back, and why.

Probability model

  • Team attack and defence strength from goals per game, shrunk toward the league average to avoid overreacting to small samples.
  • Goals drawn from a Poisson model with home advantage; overtime treated as 50/50.
  • Liiga's points and tie-break rules implemented and checked against the real standings; playoff lines read from the standings API.
  • 10,000 simulated seasons for every moment in a match replay.

Verified AI stories

The model writes words around placeholders such as {{e3.ILV.playoff_after}}; every number is filled from a fact sheet built by code. A plain-code checker (no AI) blocks a story if it contains:

  • a typed number, an unknown fact, or a fact it claims but doesn't show,
  • a name that isn't in the fact sheet,
  • more than 60 words, or banned topics such as betting tips, injuries or guarantees.

Challenges & decisions

Language models invent numbers

Challenge
A newsroom can't publish AI text that might contain a wrong statistic.
Decision
The AI never writes a number. It writes around placeholders, and a deterministic checker, not another model, decides what gets published.
Why
Rule-based checks are predictable and testable, so trust doesn't depend on a second model behaving well.

Simulation cost vs. page speed

Challenge
10,000 seasons for every goal is too slow to compute on page load.
Decision
Precompute odds, facts and stories to JSON. The web app only plays them back; the explorer's /api/simulate is the one live call.
Why
Fast pages, and an offline copy keeps the demo working even when the free backend is asleep.

Probabilities people can trust

Challenge
A wrong standings rule or an overconfident model would mislead readers.
Decision
Tie-break rules checked against real standings, the model backtested on last season, every probability labelled "Model estimate", and big swings (≥ 25 points) routed to an editor.

What I learned

  • Putting AI behind a deterministic check makes its output publishable; the guardrails mattered as much as the model.
  • Precomputing heavy work is what made a hackathon demo feel fast and reliable.

What I'd improve next

  • Stream live match events instead of replaying saved games.
  • Extend the backtest into an ongoing calibration report for the probability model.
